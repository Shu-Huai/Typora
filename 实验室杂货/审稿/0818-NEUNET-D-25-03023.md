# 0818-NEUNET-D-25-03023

This paper proposes a multi-task learning framework for mediastinal lymph node metastasis prediction based on chest CT images. The authors design two key components: a Dual-control Branch Routing Gate (DBR) to guide feature routing across tasks, and a Dual-Dimensional Gradient Balancing (DD-GB) method to mitigate gradient conflicts in multi-task learning. Overall, it is sufficiently innovative and experimental, but there are the following areas for improvement.

1. There are multiple instances in the manuscript where the full form of abbreviations such as "Dual-control Branch Routing Gate (DBR)" and "Dual-Dimensional Gradient Balancing Method (DD-GB)" are repeatedly introduced in the main text.
2. There are several spelling and grammatical mistakes in the manuscript, such as "conducte a retrospective study". Please carefully review the entire manuscript and correct these mistakes.
3. In the ablation study (Table 2), when the Feature routing gate is removed and only the Task routing gate is retained, Auxiliary Task 1, compared to the full model, exhibits a notable increase in both AUC and F1-Score, yet its accuracy (ACC) actually decreases. The authors do not provide any explanation for this phenomenon. As these metrics reflect different aspects of model performance, such inconsistencies warrant further discussion. The authors should analyze the potential causes --- not only for the observed divergence between the metrics, but also for why the full model underperforms this ablated variant on Auxiliary Task 1.
4. The manuscript mentions the use of a "public dataset of Chest CT-Scan images" for external validation, but does not provide the dataset’s specific name, source, or citation.
5. While the proposed DBR and DD-GB modules appear novel, the manuscript lacks a thorough comparison with existing related methods, particularly PCGrad, GradNorm, and Mixture of Experts (MoE)-based architectures. It remains unclear how the proposed gradient balancing mechanism (DD-GB) differs from or improves upon prior gradient manipulation techniques in multi-task learning. The authors are encouraged to provide a more detailed discussion—either in the related work or methods section—highlighting the conceptual and practical differences between their approach and existing literature. Such a comparison is essential to better demonstrate the contribution and originality of the proposed method.
