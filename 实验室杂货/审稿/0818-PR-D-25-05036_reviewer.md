# 0818-PR-D-25-05036_reviewer

This paper proposes an EEG Augmentation Network to improve emotion recognition from electroencephalography signals. Using a dataset of about 20,000 trials from 20 subjects, the authors evaluate their method with 10-fold cross-validation and conduct ablation studies to assess the contribution of the augmentation scheme. The results suggest that data augmentation enhances classification accuracy compared to baseline models. Overall, it is sufficiently innovative and experimental, but there are the following areas for improvement.

1. The caption of each figure is way too simple. The authors should provide sufficient information in the caption of each figure to make it self-explanatory. 
2. The paper mentions that the task was formulated as a binary classification problem, but in their data processing, they divided distortions into five levels. The authors did not explain the correspondence between the two, nor did they provide a reason.
3. The paper reports 10-fold cross-validation on 20,000 trials (20 subjects), but it is unclear whether the folds are subject-independent or subject-mixed. Given the strong subject-specificity of EEG, mixing trials from the same subject in both training and testing can artificially inflate performance and misrepresent generalization. The division method should be mentioned in the paper. On the other hand, Leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) is a suggested method.
4. While the ablation study shows that incorporating the EEG Augmentation Network improves performance, the effectiveness of the augmentation network itself is not directly reported. Readers may be interested in understanding how well the augmentation method alone performs, including the generation quality, the precision and other metrics.
5. The efficiency compared with other methods, given that the proposed method consists of 3 submodule, is suggested to be included in the paper.
6. There are some grammar issues in the manuscript. For example, the sentence "This study will collect EEG responses …" is inconsistent in tense. The authors are encouraged to carefully proofread the paper and ensure grammatical consistency throughout.
