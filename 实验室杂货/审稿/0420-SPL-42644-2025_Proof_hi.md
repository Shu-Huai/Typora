This paper introduces a Gaussian Mixture-based multi-center classifier to address intra-class variation in image classification. In addition, it introduces a multi-center label assignment strategy to ensure the effectiveness of each sub-center. Compared to traditional linear classifiers and some existing multi-center approaches, this method provides better performance. Overall, it is sufficiently innovative and experimental, but there are the following areas for improvement.

1. Most of the current ablation studies focus on the influence of hyperparameters, which makes the structure of this section less clear and somewhat logically inconsistent. It is recommended to separate these analyses into a dedicated subsection for hyperparameter sensitivity analysis.
2. The paper mentions the model degenerates to a linear classifier during inference to reduce computational complexity, yet there is no experiment on the computational complexity, nor the performance loss caused by this degeneration.
3. The statement "The depth feature of the sample is denoted as x." is ambiguous.
4. "The classification process can be formulated as the classifier constructs a proxy class center for each class..." is also unclear semantically.
5. Consider adding statements to show how multi-center structures reshape decision boundaries.
6. The theoretical explanations for using the Gaussian distribution are rather sketchy. It is recommended to add empirical or visual analysis of the rationality of the Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) assumptions.
